# ForgePath Build Reference

ForgePath is the working private-alpha name for the Adaptive Strength and Hypertrophy App.

## Canonical Product Sources

The authoritative product specifications live in JB's Obsidian vault:

- `Projects/Adaptive Strength and Hypertrophy App/Outputs/Adaptive Strength and Hypertrophy App Build Bible.md`
- `Projects/Adaptive Strength and Hypertrophy App/Outputs/Build Bible Requirement Traceability Matrix.md`
- `Projects/Adaptive Strength and Hypertrophy App/Inputs/App Requirements Register.md`

The public GitHub source repository stores release snapshots under [`docs/product/`](product/README.md) so the complete specification is preserved beside the implementation. Obsidian remains authoritative when a snapshot and the live vault differ.

The first implementation is governed by the complete R-001 through R-387 register. It is a deliberately bounded private-alpha slice, not a claim that every requirement or later delivery phase is complete. Its exact status is recorded in Obsidian at `Outputs/Private Alpha Implementation 2026-08-10.md`.

## Implemented Private-Alpha Slice

- Today, Plan, Progress, Library, and You navigation.
- A 242-movement canonical built-in catalog plus six-match recommendations and searchable full-library workout substitutions, including a verified squat-to-leg-press path that preserves separate exact histories and prescriptions.
- Repository-local product and design registers plus an original Today Training Field Guide that projects the current route, next earned progression target, confidence, and exact-source count into a compact handheld-inspired panel.
- A standard UI boundary gate for original-expression, technique-video exclusion, readable typography, focus, reduced motion, compact mobile support, and context-document presence.
- `life-aware-review-v1` separates external schedule disruption, readiness uncertainty, health or safety interruption, and stale exact exposure before proposing a response. It shows distinct decisions for today, the current training round, and the next athlete-approved training-block review. Its rolling 28-day plan-fit evidence reports opportunity completion, linked and unlinked completed work, protected-priority coverage, and lower-priority sets intentionally not carried forward without turning any of them into adherence punishment or volume debt.
- `ongoing-confidence-v1` keeps main-lift prescription, schedule fit, recovery response, and volume-tolerance confidence independent. Exact protected movements earn transparent zero-to-five evidence marks from repeated dates, recency, and complete effort and quality evidence; the marks are ordinal evidence strength, not probability or athlete ability. Every lane exposes its sources and one optional `Learn next` request, while skipped answers remain unknown and stale history remains visible.
- Four-stage, fully skippable starting-profile onboarding with a Quick Start route. The opening step offers Powerlifting and Hypertrophy as primary goals and pre-selects neither, so an unconfirmed goal stays an explicit unknown and routes to bridge calibration until the athlete chooses. History import remains available from Library and from the step-four placement result rather than the opening step.
- Versioned `placement-v3` inputs and independent one-to-five dimensions for experience, recent continuity, global movement skill, strength tolerance, volume tolerance, schedule stability, and data confidence.
- `movement-placement-v2` stores an exact protected movement, family context, current skill, heavy-work tolerance, evidence confidence, recommended route, athlete-selected route, reasons, unknowns, and any explicitly accepted history review without combining exact exercise history.
- `structure-progression-v1` progresses a drop set or myo-rep block against the last time the same block was performed, following the same ordering as the rest of the app: load first, then repetitions, then an added set. Total work across the whole block is the measure rather than the leading set alone, so holding the top load while completing more reps across the drops counts as progress, and so does repeating the same work with one more drop. A first block is recorded as a baseline rather than as progress, and falling total work repeats the block instead of asking for more.
- Volume and deload analysis is explicitly advisory and athlete-approved. Good-day extra work remains unrestricted, visibly athlete-authored, and included in later dose evidence with a standing fatigue warning. Straight sets protect primary and secondary work; no more than two purposeful technique suggestions appear on stable accessory or tertiary movements.
- Fresh first use contains no completed training, sessions, records, or active cycle. Established-athlete browser tests inject an explicit fixture so fake history never leaks into a real athlete account.
- The 242-movement library renders in progressive groups of 48, preserving search and filter truth without mounting every card on a phone. Collapsed chart panels do not mount hidden charts, preventing zero-size rendering warnings.
- The four earned athlete forms are original illustrated pixel art shipped as transparent images. Every form keeps the same face, hair, framing, and pixel density and changes only build and dress, so a stage reads as the same person grown rather than a different character. The design is Polynesian-inspired strength imagery expressed through invented geometry only: the banding does not reproduce tatau or any culturally specific design, and no real or mythological figure is depicted.
- Every movement in the library carries its own small original drawing instead of a shared pattern arrow. The scene is chosen from the movement name and family first and the movement pattern second, so an athlete-created movement still gets a fitting picture. The drawing is decoration only: the movement name, family, and pattern label always stay on screen.
- Number cells inside a workout keep exactly what the athlete typed while editing. Clearing a cell leaves it blank rather than snapping to zero, so the next digit reads as itself, and leaving a cell empty keeps the last committed value instead of storing zero.
- The workout clock runs from the moment the session started, which is the same stamp the finished session measures its real duration from, so there is nothing to start and nothing to forget. It stays visible on a phone, where the other header statistics are hidden.
- The exercise library filters on body part and movement type only, with a saved-movements toggle and a view-all shortcut. Training role and equipment availability were removed as filters; equipment availability still shows on every card.
- Sessions prescribe working sets only. Warm-up guidance was removed from route profiles and from the workout screen, because how an athlete prepares for their first work set is their own business and the app was never measuring it. The placement check still asks how the work felt, and only after every set of that movement is logged.
- The protected primary movement is called a main lift in every athlete-facing surface. Stored role tags and placement fields keep their existing identifiers so saved catalogs and backups stay valid.
- The level beside the athlete reads `LV` and the number, and an experience bar showing points into the current level appears in the sidebar, the Progress banner, and the You profile. Points still come only from completed work.
- Progress and You fold each section behind its heading. The heading and its own controls stay visible; only the body collapses, and the toggle is a separate button so header controls are never swallowed by a click target.
- Open-ended post-session answers are shown back to the athlete on Today under their own words. They are displayed, never rewritten, and they change no target on their own.
- The catalog carries specialty-bar, machine, suspension, bodyweight, strongman, and small-isolation work alongside the competition lifts, so a program can be built at a commercial gym, a home rack, or a hotel with a strap. Movements known by a brand keep a descriptive catalog name and hold the brand in an alias, so search finds both.
- Local persistence moves to version 25 with the catalog expansion. The migration re-merges the shipped catalog into a saved one, so an athlete already carrying the older 154-movement list receives the additions while keeping their favorites, joint-feel ratings, custom movements, and aliases.
- `session-clock-v1` starts the workout clock when the session starts and lets the athlete stop and start it. A stop is stored on the session, so it survives navigation, reload, and process death, and the stopped stretch is removed from the trained minutes the finished session records. The number on screen and the number in history are the same number.
- `training-split-v1` derives how narrow each training day should be from how often the athlete actually trains. The constant it protects is frequency per muscle, roughly two to four sessions per muscle per week: one to three days a week train the whole body each session because splitting would leave most muscles trained once, four to five days run upper and lower, and six or more give each pattern its own day. Splitting happens to keep per-muscle frequency in range as total sessions rise, not because more days should feel more serious.
- An optional local sprite pack can replace the drawn athlete forms. The pack folder is gitignored and never enters the repository or the published site, and the original drawn forms are used whenever no pack is present. The primary movement was previously called the primary anchor throughout the interface.
- `athlete-level-v1` gives the athlete a level and one of four earned forms, drawn as an original pixel athlete whose frame, limbs, and silhouette detail change with each form. A level badge sits beside the head wherever the avatar appears, including navigation. Points come only from completed sessions, confirmed and numeric records, volume actually moved, and movements trained enough to have a real history, so a level can never fall and cannot be bought. Each level costs more than the last, and the breakdown of what earned it is always shown.
- `deload-v1` follows the Renaissance Periodization prescription rather than simply cutting volume. Sets fall to the minimum effective volume, repetitions to roughly half the block's opening week, and load holds at that opening week's weight before halving for the back half. Effort stays at least four repetitions short of failure, because hard training does not remove fatigue.
- Deloads are forecast before performance slides rather than after. A block runs about five to six weeks, and weeks with no training built no fatigue so they push the timing back instead of counting toward it. Muscles losing performance, muscles at their ceiling, high end-of-session fatigue, and dropped motivation each pull the deload earlier, but the pull is capped so the block never collapses and the athlete keeps the choice of when to take it. Only genuinely past-due evidence removes that choice.
- Volume ceilings learn from the athlete. Two consecutive blocks recovered at the ceiling raise it by one set, while a single genuine failure to recover lowers it immediately, because carrying a ceiling that is too high costs far more than one conservative block. Weekly sets from a week with missed sessions are scaled per completed session before comparison, since an interrupted week and a full week are not the same measurement.
- Pump and target stimulus are asked per trained muscle rather than once per session, because volume is judged per muscle. Only muscles that received direct work are asked about, at most four per session, and quick mode asks stimulus alone. Volume progression uses the answer recorded against that exact muscle when it exists and inherits the session answer otherwise, labelling each row exact or attributed so the athlete can see which readings are measurements and which are approximations.
- The post-session survey asks how long the session actually took, which can be compared against the planned duration to calibrate future time estimates instead of leaving them a fixed guess.
- `volume-progression-v2` turns explicitly answered feedback into an athlete-approved next-round set proposal. Each muscle carries provisional landmarks for maintenance, minimum effective volume, the productive band, and recoverable ceiling, but pain, exact comparable performance, fatigue, and between-session recovery outrank those priors. Athlete-added and reduced-load technique work remain dose without earning overload. Conflicting, skipped, or insufficient feedback holds rather than guessing. A low landmark count cannot override poor recovery or falling performance, and deload or pain responses can never add work.
- Volume decisions are proposals with stated reasons and named unanswered inputs. Three or more missing answers returns insufficient evidence and holds, because adding sets on a guess is how an athlete gets buried. Pain is treated as a safety boundary rather than a volume signal, and drops and mini sets are excluded from the performance comparison so a technique week is never read as a decline.
- Pump, stimulus, and end fatigue are asked once per session while volume is judged per muscle, so a session's answers are attributed to the muscles that received direct work that day. The interface states that this is an approximation rather than a measurement.
- `set-structure-v1` records how a block of volume was actually performed. Supersets group across two movements, drop sets and myo-reps group within one, and every set in a structure shares a group id, kind, role, and position that follow the work into completed history. History can therefore answer how volume was performed, not only that it happened.
- No structure is available on the primary movement, because that is where placement verification reads its first completed set and where the exact progression clock lives. Supersets are additionally available on secondary work; drop sets and myo-reps stay on accessory and tertiary work, matching their evidence as secondary volume strategies.
- Superset pairing refuses two movements that share a primary region. Agonist-antagonist pairing is what preserves volume load and increases completed repetitions, while same-muscle pairing measurably reduces the volume load the athlete came for.
- Drops and myo-rep mini sets count fully toward volume, muscle dose, records, and exact history, but are excluded from the progression signal. They are performed at a reduced load or a truncated rep target, so comparing them as ordinary exposures would read a productive technique week as a regression. The top set and the activation set carry progression.
- A drop set built on a movement with no load target yet stays unloaded rather than inventing a starting weight.
- Training roles are a chain: primary, secondary, accessory, and optional tertiary. Secondary work is chosen to make the primary stronger, accessories to make the secondary stronger, and tertiary is extra hypertrophy or general-quality work. Time compression sheds from the bottom upward, so tertiary is cut before accessory, accessory before secondary, and the primary movement is protected last. Sessions and substitution events stored under the retired five-role vocabulary remain historical truth and are mapped forward on read rather than rejected: priority accessory becomes accessory, and both maintenance and optional become tertiary.
- Effort is read as RPE on strength-expression routes and RIR on building routes. Only RIR is stored, because RPE is the same evidence read from the other end of the same scale. That keeps one movement's history comparable across a strength block and a hypertrophy block, which the progression engine depends on, and needs no migration.
- A movement with no completed exact history is given no load target. The set shows an empty load field and the effort target carries the prescription until the athlete logs what they actually lifted, after which the next session starts from that real number instead of an invented one.
- Data confidence is derived, never self-graded. Build Bible 57.5 defines it as a count of exact recent evidence and 4297 states it describes how much evidence exists rather than how capable the athlete is, so onboarding no longer asks the athlete to rate it at either the plan level or per movement. No exact history is a definite 1 on that scale rather than an unknown. Heavy-work tolerance remains an athlete judgement that history may only suggest and the athlete must explicitly accept.
- Placement questions use plain language. Movement skill is now technique consistency, each dimension carries a one-line explanation of what it is and is not, and the protected-anchor panel reports what the athlete's logged history actually shows instead of naming an internal rule.
- Library movements show their movement pattern as an icon, a readable tag, and screen-reader text rather than the first letter of the movement name, which only repeated the name beside it.
- Every survey scale states what its endpoints mean. Direction is per question rather than universal: pain and stress are worse at the top, technique and motivation are better at the top, and session difficulty, expected comparison, end fatigue, and pump are magnitude scales where a high answer is evidence rather than a bad result.
- The protected deadlift anchor is the conventional deadlift. Sumo and paused sumo remain selectable catalog movements.
- `placement-history-v1` summarizes exact source sets inside a 42-day review window, dates, imported and numeric-only status, RIR availability, quality confirmation, latest exposure, bounded suggestions, limitations, and source-set IDs. The athlete must explicitly accept confidence or tolerance, and the engine never infers skill, pain, recovery, or family transfer.
- Deterministic introductory, reacclimation, bridge, base-building, hypertrophy, powerbuilding, strength, power, event-specific, and pain-aware route selection with explicit confidence, reasons, unknown inputs, lower and higher route comparisons, first-session verification, and criterion-based exit evidence.
- Athlete-controlled confirmation, conservative placement, faster submaximal test request, answer correction, goal change, and history import. Quick Start defaults remain visibly low-confidence until confirmed by completed work.
- Pain-modified placement carries a non-medical boundary, routes to the athlete profile, and pauses both surveyed and survey-free workout starts until reassessment.
- `placement-verification-v1` turns the first one to three normal training sessions for each exact protected movement into productive route checks without a maximal test. Sequence and quota are scoped to the exact movement lane, so evidence from squat cannot consume or confirm the bench lane. It stores optional warm-up response, the first completed primary set, planned versus actual load, repetitions and RIR, completion, duration, readiness, post-session quality, pain, time fit, and optional recovery. The movement-specific prompt stays hidden until every set of that exact movement is logged, so a placement question never interrupts the work it asks about. An unreached prompt leaves the response an explicit unknown, and the post-session pain answer remains an independent path to the same reassessment gate.
- Verification verdicts remain explainable and athlete-controlled: support the route, collect more evidence, suggest review, or require reassessment before another automatic start. No verification event silently changes the selected route.
- Deferred post-session feedback replays the same verification event from source evidence rather than creating a second interpretation.
- `placement-exit-v1` joins productive verification events only to the exact placement identity and matching cycle-level route. It evaluates two resolved checks, repeated route support, the pain-changing boundary, and supportive recovery evidence while disclosing movement-lane checks excluded because their effective route differs.
- Checkpoint recommendations are collect evidence, hold current, confirm current, review advance, review conservative, or reassessment required. The assessment stores its complete placement and verification snapshots so restore validation can replay the decision rather than trusting a label.
- `placement-exit-review-v1` requires the athlete to keep the current route, open placement reassessment, or defer with a reason. Reassessment creates a future placement and plan version; prior evidence remains historical truth. Pain-changing evidence blocks keep-current, and no recommendation silently changes programming.
- `movement-placement-exit-v1` replays one exact movement placement against only that exact identity's checks. It excludes other protected movements, family neighbors, and the global route from confirmation evidence, then evaluates two resolved checks, repeated support, the pain-changing boundary, and supportive recovery.
- `movement-placement-exit-review-v1` stores the athlete's keep, reassess, or defer decision and reason with the complete source placement, movement placement, and verification snapshots. An earned lane prompt survives queue advancement; reassessment opens the versioned placement flow and never directly rewrites current programming.
- A protected-primary substitution cancels only the active placement check for that session and placement identity. The replacement owns its completed sets and substitution ledger, the original movement's progression clock stays frozen, and the cancelled attempt consumes no exact-lane sequence or quota. Secondary and accessory substitutions do not cancel the primary check.
- `session-extension-v1` lets an athlete who feels good add sets to any movement or add another movement mid-session. Added work is stored as real training history and flagged athlete-added, so volume, muscle dose, records, and exact history all count it while plan compliance stays honest about what the engine actually prescribed. An added set repeats the last prescribed target instead of inventing a heavier one, and an added movement is always optional accessory work, never the session's primary movement. Placement verification reads prescribed sets only: an athlete-added set can never become the first source set, and completion status, planned-set count, and completion rate all measure the prescription. Extra work is refused while a session records pain that changed training, and reduced readiness produces a stated caution rather than a block.
- `calendar-exposure-v1` derives a 42-cell month view from stored planned sessions and completed source sets without persisting a second truth. Planned and actual dates remain linked through session identity and drift days, while imported or unlinked work stays visible without becoming plan compliance.
- Exact-movement exposure order groups only one canonical exercise's completed source sets, assigns chronological exposure sequence, retains calendar-day gaps, and describes load-first, repetition, set, volume, baseline, or held outcomes without borrowing family history or changing the prescription.
- Fixed-event countdowns require an explicit valid ISO date inside the athlete-authored event text. Missing or unreadable dates remain visible as missing or unreadable and never become an invented deadline.
- `missed-opportunity-v5` retains `schedule-eligibility-v1` and `schedule-readiness-v1`, then adds `schedule-priority-dose-v1` as the final evidence-backed queue tie-break before planned date. It compares completed source-set representation across athlete-declared priority regions inside a rolling 28-day window. Athlete pins, executable protected primaries, fully executable sessions, and exact-primary recency remain stronger; no optimal-dose, neglect, or catch-up-volume claim is made.
- The deterministic rebuild ranks open sessions by days since the latest completed exact primary exposure, time-fits only the first opportunity, removes optional fatigue after repeated or ongoing disruption, and keeps reported but unlogged training outside progression until it is logged or imported.
- Today explains the rebuilt order, Plan exposes the append-only decision and session changes, and Progress retains the original missed date alongside the current moved opportunity.
- `route-session-v3` applies the exact anchor's movement placement to its generated session while preserving the cycle-level goal route separately. A new squat can use skill-first work while a prepared bench uses strength work in the same cycle.
- Every trainable route retains its distinct primary, secondary, accessory, set, repetition, RIR, intensity, rest, warm-up, dose, progression, explanation, and equipment rules. Version 1 and version 2 history remains valid and unchanged.
- The selected training location removes unavailable secondary and accessory candidates before generation, applies movement-class load increments immediately, and stores the exact normalized equipment snapshot on the plan and every generated session.
- Protected strength anchors are never silently replaced. An unavailable anchor remains explicit and enters the existing review and substitution path with exact missing-item evidence.
- Onboarding regenerates only future planned or deferred sessions. Exact completed and partial history remains attached to its original plan, while every new session stores placement date, selected route, rule version, strategy, and plain-language reasons.
- Target load derives from the latest completed exact movement first, then an existing exact prescription. A movement with no exact evidence keeps zero-load calibration and never borrows weight from another variation.
- Pain-aware placement pauses automatic generation and start until movement restrictions are reassessed. This remains a programming boundary, not diagnosis or medical clearance.
- Independent full 10, quick 5, minimal 3, ask-each-time, and off modes for pre- and post-session surveys.
- Per-question answer, skip, not-sure, and prefer-not states plus immediate survey-free start and finish. Untouched defaults remain unknown rather than becoming fabricated answers.
- Optional post-session “Remind me later” finishes the workout immediately, creates a quiet 24-hour local follow-up, never blocks the next session, and expires without penalty or invented evidence.
- Explicit deferred technique and pain answers replay quality-dependent records from the original completed source sets. Dismissed or expired follow-ups leave numeric bests visibly unverified.
- Active workout logging with local persistence.
- Persistent commercial, home, travel, hotel, bodyweight, and custom training-location profiles with exact available-equipment lists, constraints, units, and separate barbell, dumbbell, cable, machine, and other load increments.
- Conservative availability matching that treats every explicit exercise requirement absent from the active profile as unavailable. Today requires an equipment review before starting a conflicting session, Workout blocks logging for unresolved movements, and Library provides available and unavailable filters with exact missing-item evidence.
- Equipment-aware substitutions that remove unavailable candidates, explain active-location fit, and retain the selected location in the substitution ledger.
- Executable workout targets rounded at session start to the smallest active-profile increment for the movement's equipment class. Actual load entry remains athlete-controlled.
- Primary, secondary builder, priority, maintenance, and optional exercise roles.
- Load-first, repetition-second, recovered-set-third progression decisions.
- Hold, reduce, and reacclimation paths.
- Time-aware session compression.
- Calendar and exposure clock presentation.
- Editable mesocycle objectives, adaptation emphasis, opportunities, time budget, exposure rounds, strength anchors, priority regions, maintenance regions, and criterion fields.
- Deterministic plan preview with protected-anchor coverage, projected sets, time fit, selection rationale, and preview-before-apply.
- Immutable mesocycle revision history with status, effective date, prior-plan link, and required reason for change.
- Future-only plan replacement that preserves completed, partial, stopped, deferred, and expired historical truth.
- Missed-session replanning without catch-up volume.
- Exact exercise history, stable canonical IDs, aliases, duplicate warnings, preferences, and joint response.
- Auditable custom-movement editing for name, family, movement type, body part, equipment, description, and aliases. Built-in taxonomy remains protected except for athlete-managed aliases.
- Exact name and alias collisions are blocked before save. Probable related variations stay visible for athlete review, prior completed-set names remain historical truth, and every catalog edit has a required reason and one-step undo.
- Initial creation of an exact duplicate requires a meaningful distinction. Probable pairs are collapsed into connected cleanup groups so multiple accidental copies can merge into one selected identity in one reason-required, reversible event.
- Completed-history CSV import requires date, exercise, load, and repetitions; previews row errors and unit conversion; auto-maps only exact canonical names or aliases; requires athlete selection for every uncertain source name; preserves file, row, original name, date, unit, and occurrence-aware fingerprint provenance; keeps quality numeric-only; and commits as one reversible ledger event.

- A durable substitution-learning ledger linking original and selected movements, candidate snapshots, prescriptions, completed source sets, outcomes, and available feedback.

- Real daily, weekly, rolling-28-day, calendar-month, calendar-quarter, yearly, and all-time filters with source-set reconciliation.
- Exclusive primary-region and upper-body, lower-body, arms, and trunk volume lenses.
- Exact-movement mix with selected-period volume, sets, repetitions, sessions, volume share, and visible limits on cross-exercise interpretation.
- Goal-relative priority attention with represented, outside-window, and no-history states. The app does not label neglect without a versioned planned-dose model.

Individual muscle rows expose contributing exercises and exact completed source-set identifiers, while upper, lower, arms, trunk, and whole-body parents conserve each source set at its highest child credit.

They are never inferred from a body-part label, remain visibly unmapped when omitted, retain review source, timestamp, and rule version, and participate in the reason-required catalog ledger and one-step undo.

Composite plan identifiers preserve repeated raw set IDs across exercise slots, unmapped plan gaps remain visible, and completed history without a stored plan stays outside compliance.

## Verified Journeys

- Complete onboarding and start directly at an experience-appropriate route.
- Open readiness, skip an individual question, and submit the remaining evidence.
- Select a minimal three-question check-in, mark one item not sure, submit two explicit answers, display low evidence confidence, set post-session surveys off, and finish directly without a modal or loss of training credit.
- Start immediately without a survey.
- Log an actual set, reload the app, and recover the active session locally.
- Explain why a primary exercise is being changed, inspect ranked evidence and tradeoffs, confirm the protected-anchor change, receive a movement-specific prescription, freeze the original exact-movement clock, and cancel the original lane's active placement check without losing replacement history.
- Finish a partial session with or without feedback and update progress only from completed sets.
- Defer post-session feedback, verify the next workout remains fully available, complete the optional follow-up later, and replay an unverified numeric best into a quality-validated record from the same completed source set.
- Navigate Today, Plan, Progress, Library, and You on desktop and a 390 px phone viewport.
- Export a complete local backup, validate and preview it, restore it, and recover the pre-restore state.
- Switch every progress horizon and prove chart, body-lens, and headline totals equal the selected completed source sets.
- Select the calendar quarter, show monthly quarter points, inspect exact movement mix and priority attention, and preserve exact-width phone containment without turning volume share into a stimulus or enjoyment claim.
- Preview a 30-minute plan revision, apply it, preserve partial history, inspect both plan versions, reload, and recover the active version from local persistence.
- Export and preview a verified version 19 backup containing replay-validated plan-route and exact-movement checkpoint reviews, exact-history reviews, per-movement placement, equipment-aware route-generated session provenance, productive verification evidence, expanded records, athlete preferences, training locations, plan versions, ledgers, survey evidence, and deferred-feedback provenance.
- Create a distinct custom movement, edit its canonical metadata, block an exact alias collision, save a non-conflicting identity without changing its stable ID, inspect the zero-volume catalog ledger entry, and undo the edit.
- Document two intentionally separate exact-name matches, review the resulting three-identity cleanup group, keep Competition Bench Press, retire both copies in one merge, verify the group disappears, and restore both identities through undo.
- Complete one set from a stored sixteen-set route-generated session, show one of sixteen linked sets, distinguish older completed sets with no stored plan, classify chest from its exact planned mappings, and preserve all unlinked work in Progress without counting it as plan compliance.
- Import a source-dated CSV with an exact alias and an unmatched legacy name, require canonical mapping before commit, preserve unverified row provenance, block a second import from duplicating volume, and undo the complete import in one action.
- Confirm a planned hold remains a hold after an athlete edits actual load, then log, provisionally recognize, save, and replay a source-backed exact-movement load record.
- Preview the original sound pack, enable sounds, turn quiet mode on, reload, and confirm both preferences persist while quiet mode suppresses playback without changing training or record calculations.
- Verify the achievement and settings surfaces on desktop and phone with no browser errors or horizontal overflow.
- Review a custom movement's direct and secondary muscle mapping, confirm the replay notice and audit description, inspect its explicit mapping in the movement detail, and undo the review without changing completed history or the stable canonical ID.
- Reconcile a stored route-generated plan to its mapped intended sets, preserve repeated raw planned-set IDs as distinct exercise-slot evidence, and keep all unlinked completed work separate from linked muscle-plan completion.

## Core Integrity Rules

1. Planned work never counts as completed work.
2. Missing survey data stays unknown.
3. Untouched interface defaults are not athlete answers.
4. Missed training earns no progression and creates no volume debt.
5. Exercise substitutions own their own completed history.
6. Gamification cannot change the training prescription.
7. The deterministic engine must function offline and without an AI provider.
8. Changes to rules, calculations, exercise identity, or evidence semantics must remain versionable and testable.
9. Deferred feedback can enrich completed evidence but cannot change whether the workout counted or block another workout.
10. Muscle-dose mappings are explicit, versioned heuristics. They are not tonnage, measured stimulus, neglect diagnoses, or automatic catch-up instructions.
